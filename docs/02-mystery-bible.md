# The Big Chill: mystery bible

This is the source of truth for the story. The build reads its structure from the content config (see 04-build-spec.md), but the intent lives here.

## The premise

Overnight, the Hanmer Springs hot pools have run ice cold. The steam is gone, the water is freezing, and the town is in a flap. This is no accident. Someone has stolen the heat, and left a taunting message behind. A bumbling but loveable police chief has run out of ideas and calls in the only detectives who can crack it: our group.

The tone is a comedy caper. Nobody is in danger, nothing is scary, and the villain is more greedy and ridiculous than menacing. Think Carmen Sandiego: colourful characters, a globe trotting confidence, and a satisfying unmasking at the end.

## What actually happened (the solution)

**The culprit is Bartholomew "Barty" Bottler**, a fast talking entrepreneur.

**His scheme:** Barty secretly installed a high pressure pump behind the pool sheds and ran a hose to a tanker truck parked up a quiet back road. He has been siphoning the famous mineral hot water to bottle and sell as ultra premium "Barty's Genuine Volcanic Spring Water" at an outrageous price. He took so much, so fast, that the pools ran cold. He did not mean to freeze the town. He just got greedy, and the town froze as a side effect.

**Why it is a fair mystery:** Barty is not the obvious villain. The obvious villain is the icy rival resort owner, who turns out to be merely unpleasant. Barty gives himself away through three physical tells left at the scene, and one witness who sold him the pump. Cross reference the clues against the five suspects and only Barty matches all of them.

## The three tells

These are discovered across the stops and pinned to the case board. The thief:

1. **Wore red and white stripes.** A red and white striped thread was snagged on the pump grate.
2. **Smelled of eucalyptus.** A minty eucalyptus scent lingered by the machine.
3. **Dropped shiny bottle caps.** Bottle caps stamped with a little "B" were scattered near the scene.

Plus two supporting facts:
- **The water was pumped out, not just cooled.** Whoever did this wanted the water itself. That points at bottling, not sabotage.
- **A pump was sold.** An inventor in town sold a high pressure pump to a man in a striped bow tie who smelled of eucalyptus.

## The five suspects

Each suspect gets a stop, an interview (branching video), an alibi or an exoneration, and a set of visible traits. Four are cleared by the evidence. One matches every tell.

### 1. Baroness Cornelia von Frost
- **Who:** Glamorous, haughty owner of a rival alpine spa called Frosthaven, in a colder town up the road.
- **Motive (apparent):** If Hanmer's pools go cold, tourists flock to hers. The obvious villain.
- **Traits:** White fur coat, ice blue gloves, a monocle, a strong scent of expensive rose perfume. No stripes. Never touches a bottle.
- **Alibi:** She was giving a live television interview promoting Frosthaven at the time of the theft. It is timestamped. She is smug about it, and mentions in passing that she "saw a grubby tanker truck sneaking up the back road."
- **Cleared by:** Wrong scent, no stripes, and a timestamped alibi.

### 2. Keith Kettle
- **Who:** A grumpy hermit who lives up the hill and is sick of the tourist crowds and noise.
- **Motive (apparent):** If the pools fail, the crowds leave and he gets his peace back.
- **Traits:** Muddy size twelve gumboots, a battered brown hat, a walking stick, and a smell of woodsmoke. No stripes.
- **Alibi:** He was up the hill chopping firewood, and his neighbour waved to him. He grumbles that he "heard a truck rumbling up the back road all morning."
- **Cleared by:** Wrong scent, no stripes, and a neighbour who saw him.

### 3. Sandra Steamwell
- **Who:** The pools manager, who discovered the crime and raised the alarm. The insider everyone suspects because she had the keys.
- **Motive (apparent):** She had access to everything. Suspicious on the face of it.
- **Traits:** Blue staff uniform, a whistle, a clipboard, a faint smell of chlorine. No stripes.
- **Alibi:** She was on camera at the front desk when the pools went cold, her keys never left her belt, and she is the one who called the chief. She is the victim here, not the villain, and becomes a helpful ally.
- **Cleared by:** Front desk camera footage and the fact that she raised the alarm.

### 4. Professor Iona Watt
- **Who:** A quirky, brilliant inventor who tinkers with strange machines in a garden workshop.
- **Motive (apparent):** She builds contraptions, and this crime needed a contraption.
- **Traits:** White lab coat, goggles pushed up on wild hair, a smell of burnt toast and solder. No stripes.
- **Alibi:** She was demonstrating a toaster that butters its own toast at the local science fair, with photos to prove it. Crucially, she remembers selling a powerful high pressure pump last week "to a charming gentleman in a red and white striped bow tie who kept offering me eucalyptus lozenges." She is the witness who points straight at Barty.
- **Cleared by:** A timestamped science fair alibi, and she is the one who fingers the buyer.

### 5. Bartholomew "Barty" Bottler  (the culprit)
- **Who:** A slick, charming entrepreneur with a briefcase full of sample bottles and a scheme for everything.
- **Motive (real):** Siphon the famous hot mineral water, bottle it, and sell it as a luxury product for a fortune.
- **Traits:** Pinstripe suit, a gold tooth, and his signature red and white striped bow tie and hatband. He constantly sucks eucalyptus throat lozenges to keep his sales voice smooth, so he smells of mint. He drops little "B" stamped bottle caps everywhere he goes.
- **Alibi:** Claims he was "meeting investors," but has no proof, and a tanker truck matching his was seen sneaking up the back road.
- **Matches:** Red and white stripes, eucalyptus, and bottle caps. All three tells. He is the thief.

## The deduction

At the end, the app shows the case board: the five suspects across the top, and the three tells down the side. The group ticks which suspect matches each tell. Only Barty matches all three. The four year old can help by spotting who has the stripy bow tie in the videos.

| Tell | von Frost | Kettle | Steamwell | Watt | Bottler |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red and white stripes | no | no | no | no | **yes** |
| Smell of eucalyptus | no | no | no | no | **yes** |
| Drops bottle caps | no | no | no | no | **yes** |

Support: the water was pumped out (points at bottling), and Watt sold the pump to the striped, minty man.

## The stops (the loop)

Six stops on a compact walkable loop in the area around the pools. Stop 1 is a fixed start at the pools frontage. Stops 2 to 6 hold the five suspect interviews and can be visited in any order, which is what makes the race and the randomised routes possible. Exact pins and geofence points are captured on the map during the build (see 04-build-spec.md). Locations are kept generic and public, never a specific private business.

### Stop 1: the pools frontage (fixed start, the crime scene)
- **Location:** Outside the thermal pools entrance on the main street, viewed from the footpath. The group does not go in.
- **Story:** The chief briefs the case on video. The four year old is sworn in as Junior Detective. A disguised "mystery thief" plays a taunting message. The group examines the scene from the street.
- **Puzzle:** A look at the real world task. The app asks the group to find the three tells at the scene: the striped thread on the grate, the minty smell, and the scattered bottle caps. Tapping each on a photo of the scene adds it to the case board and sets up the deduction.
- **Junior task:** Count something permanent and visible from the footpath, for example the flags at the entrance.
- **Evidence gained:** The three tells, and the fact that the water was pumped out.
- **Then:** The app sends the group to the interview stops. Fixed order in story mode, randomised in race mode.

### Stop 2: the village green and playground
- **Suspect:** Sandra Steamwell, on her break, distraught.
- **Puzzle:** A count from the real world, for example the number of benches or the steps up to a landmark, which unlocks the front desk camera timestamp that clears her.
- **Junior task:** Find the swings, or spot a dog.
- **Evidence gained:** Sandra cleared. She becomes an ally and hints that the water was going somewhere, not just cooling.

### Stop 3: the shops on the main avenue
- **Suspect:** Barty Bottler, all charm and deflection, chewing a lozenge. Sharp players may already clock the striped bow tie and the mint.
- **Puzzle:** A simple cipher on his flashy business card, which the app shows, or reading letters off shop signage to spell a word.
- **Junior task:** Spot someone eating an ice cream, or find a striped awning.
- **Evidence gained:** A dropped bottle cap stamped with a "B", and the fact that Barty has no real alibi.

### Stop 4: the visitor centre
- **Suspect:** Baroness von Frost, loudly badmouthing Hanmer and promoting Frosthaven.
- **Puzzle:** A read from the real world, for example counting flags or reading the opening hours sign, which reveals her timestamped television alibi.
- **Junior task:** Count the flags, or find a red car.
- **Evidence gained:** Baroness cleared, plus her sighting of a tanker up the back road.

### Stop 5: the forest walk edge
- **Suspect:** Keith Kettle, grumpy, in muddy gumboots and smelling of woodsmoke.
- **Puzzle:** A nature count, for example counting the big redwoods or the steps at the track start, which reveals his alibi.
- **Junior task:** Find the biggest tree, or spot a bird.
- **Evidence gained:** Keith cleared, plus his account of a truck up the back road all morning.

### Stop 6: the fairy door walk
- **Suspect:** Professor Watt, tinkering happily in her garden workshop.
- **Puzzle:** A matching or logic task using the pump receipt she shows, which locks in the key evidence.
- **Junior task:** Find and count the fairy doors, which suits the youngest player perfectly. An optional friendly fairy cameo can also confirm she saw the striped man.
- **Evidence gained:** Watt cleared, and the pump was sold to a man in a red and white striped bow tie who smelled of eucalyptus. This points straight at Barty.

### The accusation and finale
- **Trigger:** Once all five suspects have been interviewed, the app opens the deduction board.
- **Accuse:** The group names the culprit by matching the tells. A wrong accusation adds a time penalty in race mode. In story mode the app simply asks the group to look again.
- **The catch:** On naming Barty, the group is guided to shut off his pump. Everyone puts a finger on the screen and does the final gesture: press and hold the valve, then blow the steam back. A burst of steam fills the screen and the water is restored.
- **The reveal:** A comedy video plays. The chief nabs Barty, who whines that his water was going to make millions. The town is saved.
- **The reward:** The Junior Detective is congratulated by name and a printable certificate appears, ready for the family to print and present.

## Notes for reuse with another group

The whole mystery lives in the content config, so a second run can keep this story exactly, or swap it. Easy variations for another group:
- Rename the culprit or make the culprit an in joke about someone in that group.
- Reshuffle which suspect is guilty by moving the tells to a different character, as long as the tells still point to exactly one suspect.
- Swap the puzzle read the world targets if a stop changes.

Keep the rule intact: the tells must uniquely identify one suspect, and every other suspect must be cleared by an alibi or a tell mismatch.
